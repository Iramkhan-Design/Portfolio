03.2 · UNIVERSITY BIOMIMICRY PROJECT · AEROSPACE CONCEPT DESIGN

Grav Sweep — Black-Hole Biomimicry Space-Debris Collector

"A design that intends clearing space junk—a fictional yet physics-inspired solution to a critical real-world orbital problem. Grav Sweep is designed by following the gravitational principle of a black hole as a biomimicry project."

Designed to eliminate defunct satellites and metallic orbital fragments to support sustainable space exploration, combining an isolated micro-black-hole containment core with autonomous debris-detection sensors and a thermal melting unit.

4th Year Biomimicry Studio Principle: Black Hole Accretion & Hawking Radiation Deliverables: 3D Orbital Render + Orthographic Blueprints
01 · CORE TECHNOLOGY & CONTAINMENT SYSTEM

Controlled Micro-Black Hole & Orbital Mimicry

  • Core Technology: Uses a controlled micro-black hole to attract and absorb high-velocity space junk. The machine and its octagonal containment unit mimic the black hole's orbital path to prevent self-consumption and optimize debris attraction.
  • Octagonal Containment System: Ensures the micro-black hole remains stable and electromagnetically isolated, preventing unintended gravitational consequences during orbital operation.
  • Autonomous Satellite Features: Equipped with forward-facing LiDAR/radar sensors, 3 conical intake funnels, and autonomous navigation thrusters for debris detection and approach.
02 · DEBRIS COLLECTION PROCESS & HAWKING RADIATION LIFECYCLE

Capture, Neutralization & Sustainable Recall

  • Step 1 — Gravitational Attraction: The micro-black hole draws metallic fragments and defunct satellite debris into the conical intake opening.
  • Step 2 — Sensor Isolation: Once captured, optical/mass sensors immediately close the octagonal containment unit, isolating the black hole core.
  • Step 3 — Thermal Neutralization: Captured debris is transferred to the lower Melting Unit for compaction and neutralization.
  • Lifecycle & Hawking Radiation: According to Stephen Hawking, black holes emit radiation and eventually vanish. Once the onboard micro-black hole evaporates, the satellite is recalled to Earth, repaired, and redeployed.

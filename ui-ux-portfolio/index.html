01 · PROBLEM STATEMENT & LEGACY FRICTION

Simplifying an Enterprise Direct-Selling Ecosystem

Modicare serves millions of independent Advisors and everyday consumers across diverse digital literacy levels in India. The legacy web and mobile experience suffered from:

  • Onboarding Confusion: First-time visitors struggled to understand the difference between joining as a business Advisor versus shopping as a Preferred Customer (PC).
  • High-Friction Consultant Tasks: Routine back-office tasks in the Modiway portal—such as submitting PAN card KYC, updating registered mobile/email via OTP, and sharing referral links—were buried in dense menus.
  • Cluttered Catalog Discovery: Mobile shoppers faced visual fatigue navigating hundreds of SKUs across Auto Care, Beauty, Laundry, Kitchen, Wellness, and Agriculture.
02 · TARGET AUDIENCE & USER ARCHETYPES

Designing for Two Interconnected User Groups

Our information architecture had to balance enterprise business tools with consumer-grade e-commerce simplicity:

  • Independent Modicare Advisors (Consultants): Need rapid Sponsor MA/Mobile verification, drag-and-drop PAN compliance, 1-click WhatsApp/SMS referral link generation, and motivational training audio (The Samir K. Modi Collection).
  • Preferred Customers (Everyday Shoppers): Want transparent pricing (MRP vs. DP savings up to 25%), rich A+ product education, quick category filtering, and visible trust assurances (100% Love It Guarantee).
03 · KEY INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE & ACCESSIBILITY UPGRADES

What We Re-Engineered Across Desktop Web & Mobile App

  • Side-by-Side Onboarding Decision Matrix: Designed the "Welcome To Modicare Family!" landing page with parallel benefit cards (Retail Savings, Loyalty Rewards Program, Product Offers, Business Opportunity) and a checkbox fallback ("I don't have any sponsor No.") to prevent drop-offs.
  • 3-Step Modiway Self-Service Flows: Introduced a numbered 3-step visual progress bar (1. Send OTP → 2. Enter Details → 3. Confirm) for Email/Mobile updates, a clean 25MB drag-and-drop upload zone for PAN verification, and instant WhatsApp/SMS share triggers for referrals.
  • A+ Visual Product Storytelling (PDP): Upgraded product pages (e.g., Fresh Moments Fluoride Toothpaste) with a 5-stage visual infographic stack explaining active ingredients (Freshmint, SPA, Xylitol) and family oral-care benefits alongside 1-click Similar Products cross-selling.
  • Thumb-Friendly Mobile App Navigation: Created a persistent 5-icon floating bottom navigation bar paired with high-contrast sky-blue Shop pills (Categories, Brands, New Launches, Best Sellers, Luxe, Hamper) and downloadable digital catalogues (Sapne, Urban Color, Samir Modi Infinity Plan).

01 · THE USER PROBLEM

Why Pet Care Breaks Down for Vulnerable & Busy Owners

Companion animals dramatically improve mental well-being, yet three groups face severe friction in daily pet care:

  • Older Adults with Physical Limitations: Struggle with physically demanding tasks like dog walking, heavy litter/food transport, or traveling to distant veterinary clinics.
  • Time-Strapped Professionals: Need reliable daytime grooming/walking services and fast recurring nutrition orders without juggling 4 separate apps.
  • First-Time Pet Parents: Experience high anxiety around symptoms, nutrition, and vaccinations, needing trustworthy tutorials and immediate access to verified vets.
02 · UX ARCHITECTURE & ACCESSIBILITY DECISIONS

Designing a Low-Cognitive-Load Super-App

From low-fidelity wireframing to high-fidelity visual design, every screen prioritizes clarity and reassurance:

  • Persistent 5-Tab Bottom Navigation: Instant switching between Home, Categories, Offers, Pharmacy, and Vet/Clinic so elderly users never get lost in deep hierarchies.
  • Dual Vet Discovery (Screen 05): Combines "Vets Near You" (e.g., Vetic Pet Clinic with direct call & Google Maps deep-link) and "Meet Our Online Doctors" (Dr. Sasha Roy & Dr. Nina Kashyap with years of experience & specialization).
  • Transparent E-Commerce & Reward Points (Screens 07–10): Clear itemized pricing, automatic coupon/reward point deduction, and familiar Indian payment rails (UPI, COD).

✦ CASE STUDY 03 · ACCESSIBILITY & HUMAN-CENTERED INTERACTION UX (TEAM KARIGAR)

Intuitive Lift Design — Inclusive Multi-Sensory Elevator Interface

"A group project that concerns all rather than some. There are plenty of designs for normal people, but what about the disabled? Members of Team Karigar came together to eliminate this gap by designing an intuitive lift control system that can be accessed by all."

PHASE 01 · DISCOVERY & PERSONAL EMPATHY ORIGIN (IRAM'S POV)

Observing Navigating Lifts with My Grandmother

The project began from personal lived observation accompanying my grandmother in residential and commercial elevators:

  • Lived Insights: She possessed heightened auditory/tactile senses and a sharp memory, and wanted to know everything happening around her—yet current lifts forced her into complete dependence on others.
  • Core Anxiety Triggers: "I can't tell if the door is closing until it hits me" (5-second door timers) and "I panic when the lift skips floors without warning" (lack of audio/tactile floor announcements).
  • Braille Inefficiency: Flush buttons caused accidental clicks, and standard Braille plates were poorly positioned or unreadable for users who lost vision later in life.
PHASE 01B · INDUSTRY BENCHMARKING & FIELD RESEARCH IN INDIA

Why Current Elevator Innovations Still Fail Inclusivity

We audited global elevator innovations (Hitachi & OTIS) alongside primary field observations in Indian buildings:

  • Hitachi Touchless Buttons: Reduces virus transmission via IR proximity, but lacks physical tactile confirmation for blind users.
  • OTIS Destination Dispatch: Improves traffic efficiency via lobby touchscreens, but flat glass touchscreens are unusable for blind and low-vision passengers without external retrofits.
  • 3 Critical Gaps in Indian Elevators: (1) No enforced Indian accessibility guidelines for lifts, (2) Unreachable high buttons for wheelchair users & hands-full amputees, (3) Zero directional audio cues ("Are we going UP or DOWN?").
PHASE 02 · DEFINE PHASE — EMPATHY & USER PERSONAS

Three Representative Accessibility Personas

PERSONA 01 · TOTALLY BLIND FLOOR 1

Arjun Mehta

24 yrs · Master's Student in Social Work · Visually Impaired

Arjun is a college student pursuing a Master's degree in Social Work. He lost his vision at the age of 10 due to a genetic condition and navigates campus buildings independently.

Needs & Interaction Challenges
  • Struggles with finding flush elevator buttons quickly on crowded panels.
  • Prefers voice-assisted systems and distinct physical mechanics over flat touchscreens.
  • Needs immediate, unmistakable audio feedback to confirm floor selection and direction.
PERSONA 02 · ELDERLY LOW-VISION FLOOR 2

Rohan

68 yrs · Retired School Principal · Partial Blindness (Glaucoma)

Rohan is a retired school principal who developed partial blindness due to glaucoma. He moves with a walking cane and relies heavily on high-contrast environmental cues.

Needs & Interaction Challenges
  • Needs large, well-spaced tactile controls with high-contrast illuminated numbers.
  • Requires slow-closing doors (8+ seconds) and proximity sensors to avoid accidental bumps.
  • Prefers an emergency assistance feature that can be activated by voice without hunting for tiny alarm buttons.
PERSONA 03 · PROSTHETIC LIMB & MOBILITY FLOOR 3

Neha Verma

30 yrs · Software Engineer · Left Leg Amputee (Prosthetic Limb)

Neha lost her left leg in an accident and uses a prosthetic limb. She works as a software engineer and commutes daily while carrying a laptop bag.

Needs & Interaction Challenges
  • Requires a wide elevator entrance (950mm+) and 1800×1800mm cabin space for easy turning.
  • Needs a comfortable perimeter handrail at an ergonomic support height.
  • Benefits from lever-actuated, motion-sensor, or voice-operated controls when her hands are occupied holding bags or stabilizing herself.
PHASE 02B · DEFINE PHASE — END-TO-END EXPERIENCE MAPPING

5-Stage Elevator User Journey Map

Journey Stage 1. Find Lift 🚪 2. Call Elevator 🔘 3. Select Floor 🔢 4. Ride Experience 🧘 5. Exit 🚶‍♀️
User Actions Search for elevator in building lobby; locate entrance. Approach landing panel; press call button; orient toward arriving cabin. Enter cabin; locate control panel layout; select desired floor number. Stand stably inside cabin; listen/watch for floor progression. Verify target floor arrival; dash or step out before doors close.
Pain Points Poor directional signage; lack of auditory beacons in large lobbies. Unresponsive flush buttons; accidental clicks; no UP/DOWN audio confirmation. Confusing multi-column button grids; dim lighting; unreadable Braille plates. Zero floor announcements; anxiety when lift skips floors silently. Aggressive 5-second door timer slams doors on cane/wheelchair/prosthetic users.
UX Opportunities High-contrast lobby signage paired with directional voice/chime beacons. Tactile Up/Down lever + touchless wave sensors with distinct UP/DOWN audio tones. Detented rotary floor dial with notched ridges, confirmation click & giant LED numeral. Synchronized bilingual audio announcements + overhead high-contrast digital displays. Extended 8-second door hold timer + full-height proximity curtain sensors.
Expectations vs. Reality Expectation: Easy to locate elevator. Reality: Often hidden or unclear without sighted help. Expectation: Press call → clear feedback. Reality: Confusion between Up & Down buttons. Expectation: Instant tactile & visual response. Reality: Flush buttons lag, lack contrast, or malfunction. Expectation: Smooth, predictable ride. Reality: Uncertain which floor the cabin is passing. Expectation: Safe, unhurried exit. Reality: Stressful time pressure from 5s doors.
